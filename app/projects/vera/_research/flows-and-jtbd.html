User Flows & Jobs to Be Done

How the
work actually
moves.

Eleven jobs, eleven flows. A working specification of every meaningful path through the product — written around the progress the user is trying to make, not the features that would deliver it.

Rio de Janeiro · For internal product strategy · To be revised after field interviews

A flow is not a feature. A flow is the path a person walks to make a piece of their life better, briefly. Software is just the surface they happen to be walking on.

This document organizes the product's surface around jobs — moments of intended progress — rather than around the things we will build. Each job is followed by the flow that delivers it. Where two jobs share a flow, the flow appears once. Where a flow exists primarily to support a system rather than a user (notifications, reminders, the WhatsApp layer), it is grouped at the end.

The structure follows three premises from the prior research:

One. The doctor's job is to practice medicine; everything the software does for her is, ultimately, in service of removing administrative load from that practice.

Two. The patient's job is to receive care with the least possible friction; everything the software does for her is in service of certainty — that the appointment exists, that she will be reminded, that she can change it.

Three. The product is a tool, not a directory. Its loyalty is to the doctor, who pays for it. The patient experience exists because patient delight is the doctor's marketing engine.

Every flow in this document should fail one test if it does anything that does not serve the job. The test is severe and intentional.

Part One

The Doctor.

Job № 01

Get my practice online quickly and credibly.

JTBD When I decide it is time to have a real online presence, I want to look professional within minutes — not days — so that I can share my page the same afternoon and stop sending my Instagram bio as a substitute for a website.

The first session with the product is the entire pitch. If the doctor does not have a shareable, presentable page within two minutes of signing up, she has decided — silently, irreversibly — that the product is not for her.

Flow F·01 — OnboardingTarget: 2 min
Sign-up to live profile.
  1. Land & sign in Doctor arrives via referral, ad, or organic search. Single screen with two options: continue with Google, or email. No password creation on signup. CopyTu consultorio online, en dos minutos.
  2. Name & specialty Doctor enters her name and selects specialty from a list. The slug auto-generates from the name (e.g., menura.app/dra-sofia-ramirez), with one-click edit if she prefers a shorter variant. SystemSlug uniqueness checked live; collisions get a numeric suffix or alternate suggestion.
  3. First service A single service is created with three fields: name (e.g., "Consulta inicial"), duration, and price. The product refuses to advance without at least one service — this is the entire commercial surface.
  4. Availability — visual grid A week grid pre-populated with sensible defaults (Mon–Fri, 09:00–18:00). Doctor adjusts by tapping cells. No timezone questions; the product infers from device and shows it as confirmation. EdgeIf device timezone differs from the slug country expectation (e.g., Mexican doctor signing up while traveling), surface a one-line confirmation rather than a dialog.
  5. Connect WhatsApp Doctor enters the WhatsApp number where she wants to receive booking notifications. A test message is sent on confirm. This is the most important single field in the flow. CopyAquí recibirás cada nueva reserva. Sin descargar nada.
  6. Page live — share screen The public URL is now active. Doctor sees her page in a preview frame and three primary actions: Copiar enlace, Compartir en Instagram, Ver mi página. Optional secondary: Agregar foto y bio. CopyListo. Tu página está en vivo en menura.app/dra-sofia-ramirez.

Deferred — post-onboarding

Photo upload, bio, additional services, location/address or "consulta online" toggle, custom branding, multiple availability rules. None of these block first-booking-ready state. They are surfaced as gentle nudges on the dashboard after first sign-in.

Job № 02

Know what my week looks like at a glance.

JTBD When my workday begins, I want to see today's appointments in one glance — names, times, services — without scrolling, scanning, or thinking, so that I can mentally prepare for who I am about to see.

This is the most-used screen in the product. It must be readable in two seconds, on a phone, in the elevator on the way up to the consultorio.

Flow F·02 — Daily dashboardMost-used screen
Open app to today's view.
  1. Open — land on today No splash, no greeting, no metrics. Today's appointments in a vertical list. Each row: time, patient first name + last initial, service, duration.
  2. Tap an appointment Expands inline to show patient phone, reason for visit, notes from prior visits (if any), and three actions: call, WhatsApp, mark complete.
  3. Swipe horizontally — day navigation Left/right swipe moves between days. A small week strip at the top shows which days have bookings (subtle dot indicator).
  4. Switch to week view Tap to toggle. Week grid showing booking density. Useful for the doctor planning the next week's marketing push or knowing when to block time.
  5. Next available slot — passive widget Persistent footer line: "Próximo hueco libre: jueves 11:00". A signal of demand health. When this line says "esta tarde", the doctor knows it is time to post.
Design Note The dashboard's restraint is the feature. No metrics, no graphs, no engagement loops. The doctor opens the app to find out what is happening today, and that information is the first thing she sees. Everything else is a setting.
Job № 03

Receive new bookings without context-switching.

JTBD When someone books with me, I want to know immediately — but I do not want to confirm, accept, or respond — so I can stay focused on the patient in front of me.

The default behavior of the system is to confirm bookings instantly without any doctor action required. This is a deliberate inversion of the WhatsApp status quo, where every booking is a negotiation that requires the doctor's active attention.

Flow F·03 — New booking receivedPassive · zero-action
Booking happens; doctor is informed.
  1. Patient confirms — system commits The slot is reserved atomically on the database side. Race conditions handled by a uniqueness constraint, not the UI. SystemPostgres unique constraint on (doctor_id, start_time). Conflicting requests fail gracefully — patient sees "Ese horario acaba de tomarse, elige otro."
  2. WhatsApp message to doctor Within seconds: a structured WhatsApp message with patient name, service, day & time, and a link to view in the app. TemplateNueva cita: Laura García · Consulta inicial · jueves 11 de mayo, 16:00. Ver detalles ›
  3. Push notification (if app installed) Same content, secondary channel. WhatsApp is canonical because it is where the doctor lives; push is a backup.
  4. No action required The booking is real. The patient has already received her own confirmation. The doctor's involvement is optional — read the message, do nothing, the appointment exists.

Optional — approval mode

A setting (off by default) lets the doctor require manual approval for first-time patients. In approval mode, new patients book a "solicitud" rather than a confirmed slot; the slot is held for two hours pending the doctor's approval, then released. This exists for the doctor with a specific clinical reason to vet patients — most do not need it.

Job № 04

Protect my time when I am unavailable.

JTBD When I take vacation, attend a conference, or simply want a Friday off, I want to stop accepting bookings during that window without rebuilding my whole availability schedule.
Flow F·04 — Block timeQuick, reversible
Mark a range as unavailable.
  1. From calendar, tap-and-hold or long-press a date A bottom sheet appears: "Bloquear este día" / "Bloquear varios días" / "Bloquear horario específico".
  2. Select range & optional reason Range is visual — tap end date. Reason is internal-only, used for the doctor's reference (e.g., "Congreso CDMX"). Patients see nothing.
  3. Existing bookings — handled separately If the blocked range contains bookings, the system surfaces them with a clear action: "Tienes 3 citas en este rango. Notifica a tus pacientes para reagendar." A pre-written WhatsApp message is offered. CopyHola Laura, lamento avisarte que tendré que cerrar el consultorio el jueves 12. ¿Podemos mover tu cita? Aquí tienes mis siguientes huecos disponibles: [enlace]
  4. Public page reflects change immediately Slots in the blocked range disappear from the booking surface. Patients trying to book see only valid options.
V1.5 — Google Calendar Sync Two-way sync with Google Calendar so external events automatically block availability. High-leverage feature, but adds OAuth complexity to v1. Deferred.
Job № 05

Hand off changes to the patient, not the calendar.

JTBD When a patient needs to reschedule, I do not want a five-message back-and-forth. I want the patient to handle it through the system, and I want to be informed only of the outcome.

This is mirrored on the patient side as Job 09. From the doctor's perspective, the flow is purely receptive.

Flow F·05 — Reschedule (doctor view)Passive
Patient reschedules; doctor is informed.
  1. Patient uses tokenized reschedule link From her original confirmation message. No login required — the token authenticates the booking.
  2. System updates booking atomically Old slot released, new slot taken. Calendar reflects change in real time.
  3. Doctor receives WhatsApp summary A single line: "Laura García movió su cita: jueves 16:00 → viernes 11:00."
  4. No required action The doctor does not approve. She does not respond. The system has handled the entire transaction. This is the product's most important promise to the doctor.
Job № 06

Reduce no-shows.

JTBD When patients fail to show up, I lose money I cannot recover. I want a system that reduces this without making me feel like I am chasing people.

The no-show problem is solved in two layers: reminders (V1, universal) and deposits (V1.5, optional). Both are designed to operate without the doctor having to ask.

Flow F·06 — No-show preventionAutomatic
Reminders before, deposits behind.
  1. T-24h reminder WhatsApp message to patient: appointment details, location or video link, reschedule/cancel links. Same template, every patient. The doctor never types it. TemplateHola Laura, recordatorio: mañana jueves a las 16:00 tienes tu Consulta inicial con Dra. Sofía. Ubicación: Av. Álvaro Obregón 145. ¿Necesitas reagendar? Aquí ›
  2. T-2h reminder (optional) Lighter touch, useful for high-no-show categories (psicología, fisio). Per-doctor toggle.
  3. After: mark complete or no-show From the appointment row in the dashboard, the doctor taps "completada" or "no asistió". The latter is silent — no message to the patient — but informs the system for future analytics and (V1.5) deposit retention.
  4. V1.5 — optional deposit at booking Per-service toggle. If on, patient is asked to leave a deposit (Stripe or Mercado Pago) at booking time. Deposit is refunded automatically on cancellation more than 24h prior; retained otherwise.
Part Two

The Patient.

Job № 07

Decide whether to book — without an awkward DM.

JTBD When I land on a doctor's page, I want to know — in under thirty seconds — whether she has availability that works for me, what it costs, and where the consultation takes place, so I can decide whether to book without committing to a conversation first.
Flow F·07 — Land & evaluateTarget: 30 sec
Land on profile, decide.
  1. Above the fold — the decision frame Doctor's photo, name, specialty, location (or "Online"), and a single primary CTA: Reservar cita. Below the CTA, a subtle "Próxima disponibilidad: mañana 10:00".
  2. Services — price, duration, what is included Each service as a card: name, duration, price in MXN, one-line description. Tap to book that specific service. ExampleConsulta inicial · 60 min · $1,200 · Primera sesión con evaluación completa.
  3. About — bio & credentials A short, doctor-written bio. Cédula profesional (where applicable). Years of practice. Languages.
  4. Location or modality If in-person: address, map, parking note. If online: which platform (Zoom, Google Meet) and what to expect.
  5. Trust signals — passive Instagram handle link (if the doctor has provided one), specialty certifications, optional testimonials. No competitor ads. No "doctores similares" section. Ever.
Job № 08

Book quickly, with certainty.

JTBD When I have decided to book, I want the appointment to exist within sixty seconds — without creating an account, downloading an app, or waiting for someone to respond.
Flow F·08 — Book a slotTarget: 60 sec · No account
From tap to confirmation.
  1. Tap service or CTA Bottom sheet (mobile) or modal (desktop) opens. The selected service is pre-filled.
  2. Pick a date Horizontal calendar strip showing days with available slots. Days with no availability are visibly dimmed but not hidden.
  3. Pick a time Vertical list of available time slots for the selected day. Time slots in patient's local timezone, with a one-line confirmation that the doctor is in [X timezone]. EdgeIf the doctor's slots span a DST change, the system shows local-to-local mapping. Edge case but real for Mexico–US patients.
  4. Three fields, one optional Nombre · Teléfono (WhatsApp) · Email. Optionally: "¿Algo que la doctora deba saber antes?" Three fields and a button. That is the entire form.
  5. Confirm Immediate confirmation screen with appointment details, doctor's address or online link, and "Agregar a calendario" (Apple/Google). CopyTu cita está confirmada. Te enviamos los detalles a tu WhatsApp.
  6. WhatsApp message arrives Within seconds: same details, plus tokenized reschedule and cancel links. The message is now the patient's record. No need to remember anything.
Why no account Account creation is the single largest drop-off in booking flows. The product avoids it by anchoring identity to the booking itself (via phone + email) and authenticating subsequent actions via tokenized links. A returning patient is recognized by phone number on next booking, with pre-filled fields.
Job № 09

Change my plans without social cost.

JTBD When something comes up, I want to reschedule or cancel without having to compose a polite message to the doctor — because that small social friction is exactly what makes me ghost instead.
Flow F·09 — Patient reschedule/cancelTokenized, no auth
From the confirmation message.
  1. Tap "Reagendar" or "Cancelar" in WhatsApp message Opens browser to a tokenized URL specific to this booking. No login. No "verify your identity" step.
  2. Reschedule — same booking flow, pre-filled Patient sees available slots and picks a new one. Old slot is released; new one is taken. Confirmation updates.
  3. Cancel — one confirmation step "¿Confirmas la cancelación de tu cita del jueves 11 a las 16:00?" Single-tap confirm. Done.
  4. Doctor is informed; patient is free Both sides receive a brief WhatsApp summary of the change. No additional communication required.
Job № 10

Show up to the right place at the right time.

JTBD When my appointment is approaching, I want to be reminded — once, clearly, with everything I need — so I do not have to dig through old messages to find the address or the video link.
Flow F·10 — Reminder & arrivalAutomatic
The reminder is the product.
  1. T-24h reminder via WhatsApp Full context: doctor name, date, time, address or video link, and reschedule/cancel links one tap away.
  2. Optional T-2h reminder Per-doctor setting. For higher no-show categories.
  3. Calendar entry already present From the booking confirmation. The reminder is redundant by design — the patient has at least two surfaces telling her the appointment exists.
Job № 11

Trust this professional before I commit.

JTBD When I am considering a new doctor — especially in a sensitive specialty — I want to feel confident before I share my information, so I do not waste my session on someone who is not a fit.

Trust is a horizontal concern, not a discrete flow. It is the product of every other flow combined with the visual quality of the profile. Where flows touch the trust surface directly, the design must defer to the doctor's brand.

Flow F·11 — Trust signalsPassive · embedded
Where trust enters the product.
  1. Visual quality of the profile page The single largest trust signal. The profile must look like a personal site, not a directory entry. Typography, photography, generous space.
  2. Cédula profesional, displayed cleanly Where applicable — psicología, medicina, nutrición. A small badge near the doctor's name, with the cédula number on tap.
  3. Optional verified Instagram link Many patients arrive via IG. Confirming the doctor's IG handle on the page closes the trust loop — same person, same brand.
  4. Doctor-written bio, in her voice Not a template. The bio is the closest thing to meeting her before meeting her. The product must make writing it feel like writing a Substack post, not filling a form.
  5. Testimonials — post-PMF, with care Reviews introduce moderation surface, incentive to game, and emotional weight in a healthcare context. Out of scope for V1. When eventually added: opt-in per patient, doctor-curated display, no scoring system.

The System.

Flow F·12

The notification layer.

WhatsApp is not an integration. It is the operating channel of the product. Every state change in a booking has a corresponding WhatsApp event, with email as fallback and push as secondary.

Template philosophy

Every template is short, in Mexican Spanish by default, and written to sound like a thoughtful assistant — not a marketing email and not a clinical notification. The voice is closer to a competent secretary than to a brand. No emojis in transactional messages; one emoji maximum in reminders if it serves clarity (a calendar emoji before the date, for example).

Doctor-customizable copy is V2 — for V1, the templates are fixed and excellent.

WhatsApp Business API — operational reality Mexico-specific friction: Meta verification, template approvals (utility templates approve in 24–48h, marketing ones can take longer or be rejected). Twilio is the fastest path to v1 (higher per-message cost). 360dialog or Gupshup is the path to scale (cheaper, slower onboarding). Plan two weeks of buffer for first-time template approval.
Flow F·13

Edge cases & failure modes.

The flows above describe the happy paths. These are the unhappy ones — each with a designed-for response, not a thrown error.

Slot taken between page load and submit

Patient picks a slot, takes thirty seconds with the form, submits. In the meantime another patient has booked the same slot. The system fails the second request gracefully and surfaces the next two adjacent slots with a one-tap selector. The patient does not start over.

Patient without WhatsApp

Rare in Mexico but real. The booking form's phone field accepts any number; if WhatsApp delivery fails, the system falls back to SMS via Twilio (V1) or email-only (V1 minimum). The doctor is not informed of the channel difference — the booking exists either way.

Doctor's WhatsApp Business API not yet provisioned

During onboarding, while Meta verification is pending, the doctor receives booking notifications by email and push. WhatsApp activates once approval clears. The doctor's profile is fully functional throughout — verification is invisible to the patient.

Reschedule into a now-blocked time

Patient holds an old reschedule link; doctor has since blocked that range. The link still works but shows only currently available slots. If no slots are available within a reasonable window, the link offers a "Contact the doctor directly" fallback with a pre-written WhatsApp draft to the doctor's number.

Double-booking race condition

Handled at the database layer via a Postgres unique constraint on (doctor_id, start_time). The application logic catches the constraint violation and surfaces the standard "slot taken" UI. Never trusts the frontend's slot list to be current.

Doctor deletes account

V1: account deletion suspends the profile (returns 410 Gone) and notifies all future-dated patients via WhatsApp with the doctor's chosen message. Bookings within the next 48 hours trigger a special-case template. Deferred for V1.5: full data export.

What this document is not

This is not a screen-by-screen specification. It is a description of the progress users are trying to make and the paths the product offers them to make it. The screens themselves will be designed against these flows — directly in code, following the design principles in Document № 001.

Each flow above should be revisited after the first five doctor interviews. Where a flow assumes a behavior that does not show up in the field, it changes. Where the field reveals a job not described here, a new flow is added.

The job is not to build the product correctly. The job is to know what the product is for. Everything else compiles.
