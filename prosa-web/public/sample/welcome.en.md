# How this works

This is Prosa. It's a reader for essays, and it does one thing: keep long texts and put you back exactly where you left off.

There's no account to create, no onboarding to skip, nothing to configure before you start. You're already reading.

## What you can do

Paste an essay with the import button, or drag `.md` files onto this window. You can drop twelve at once.

Highlight any sentence by selecting it. Add a note and a small amber dot appears in the margin, so you can find it again later.

Tap the middle of the screen to bring up the top bar. That's where the contents and the typography live: serif or sans, size, line height, and four reading themes.

## Where your library lives

Here, in this browser. Not on a server.

That has a consequence worth saying plainly: if you clear this site's data, your library goes with it. Which is why Settings has a button to export everything as a `.zip` — your essays, your positions, your highlights — that you can import back here or into another browser.

It's the only promise Prosa can keep without asking you for anything.

## The position

Close this tab halfway through a long essay. Come back tomorrow. You'll be on the same sentence.

And if you change the text size in between, still. The position isn't stored as "this many pixels from the top", which would stop meaning anything the moment something shifts. It's stored as the paragraph you were in and how much of it you'd already passed.

It even survives re-importing an essay after editing it: if the paragraph moved, it gets found; if it changed a little, it still gets recognised; and if it's gone entirely, you land nearby and get told, instead of being sent back to the beginning in silence.

## Without internet

After the first visit, Prosa opens offline. The essays are stored in your browser rather than downloaded from anywhere, so you can read and highlight on a plane.

You can install it as an app from your browser's menu, if you'd like it to have its own icon.
